# Reflection on Final Project and Course, generally

For my final project, I analyzed heating systems in greenhouses, focusing on electric, liquid propane/gas, and biomass systems. I appreciated being able to choose a topic that is relevant and interesting to the work I engage in outside of academia, even though it is not necessarily an Electric Energy System. In connection to the course, I was able to apply energy conversion and modeling concepts learned this semester.

An area of growth for me was designing my own computational model. In EE3315 and BME 3340 (Computational Systems Biology), we were given parameters and assumptions for a model based on provided literature and asked to analyze or modify it. In this project, I had to design my own by finding equations and parameters based on literature. I learned how many factors could influence heating demand (insulation, solar gain, weather patterns, fuel price variability), and I had to make decisions about where to simplify. Even though I wanted to include more realism in my analysis, I realized that modeling is inherently about making reasonable assumptions due to time and data limitations.

The research component of the project connected unexpectedly well with another course I am taking this semester, CEE 2130, in which we learn how to economically analyze engineered systems. Many of the UVM Extension publications I looked at report results using economic metrics like payback period, cost-benefit ratio, and cash flows. Because of CEE 2130, I felt well equipped to interpret such figures and incorporate these concepts into my own analysis. Integrating some economic analysis into this project helped this project feel more useful and reminded me that there is value in didactic instruction when I can apply it to real-world systems.

Regarding the optional secondary goal of teamwork, I found myself wondering if I would have gotten more out of the project/course if I were more connected to my peers. I learn best when exchanging ideas with people of different backgrounds and realized this is a skill I would like to develop further.
