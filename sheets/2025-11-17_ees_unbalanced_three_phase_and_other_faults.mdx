## Unbalanced case

In the unbalanced case, one of the loads is halved; other parameters are the same as the balanced case. The current waveform graphs are shown in Figure 3 (Y-connected) and Figure 4 (delta-connected). For the unbalanced case, the smallest impedance has the largest phase current. Waveforms have different magnitudes. This is expected because unbalanced phase results in unbalanced currents. We have assumed the neutral is grounded for the Y unbalanced load. Without grounding, there would be unequal phase voltages and more noticeable current imbalances.

## Line-to-line fault

In the line-to-line fault, a tree branch creates a short between two lines (A and B). The power dissipated in the tree branch would be 43.264 kW (assuming V = 208 V<sub>rms</sub> and R = 1 Ω). The currents of A and B would be larger than the third line C, so there would be an imbalance in the generator.

## Connection fault

In the scenario where phases are swapped when connecting two synchronized generators, the system would be out of phase, and the voltages would be different. In the given circuit, resistor A dissipates 0 W, resistors B and C each dissipate 43.264 kW.

## Summary

Balanced 3-phase loads produce equal currents in all phases. When loads are unbalanced, currents have unequal magnitudes. Line-to-line faults cause large currents in the shorted phases which causes generator imbalance and possible damage. In a connection fault, the phases are no longer in sync which results in uneven power through each line.
