# L05: Solar PV Feasibility Study

## Executive Summary

In this study, we evaluate the economic feasibility of a 50 MW solar PV array over a 20-year outlook using dispatch logic based on Champlain Valley hourly solar radiation data in 2022 and VT 2022 electricity prices. We model the solar power plant using the Panasonic HIT PV module N330E tilted at the angle 44.5 degrees and facing south.

To achieve a 50 MW average output, the solar PV plant will be sized at 270 MW DC (262 MW AC), requiring 819,703 solar panels spanning 338.26 acres. The capacity factor is 0.19055. For solar PV, energy dispatch occurs when irradiance (as predicted by 2022 data) is greater than 0.

In one year, the plant's revenue from electricity sales sums to approximately $41.8 million per year. Operation and maintenance would cost approximately $3.9 million per year. For a 20-year loan, at i = 0.05, with principal to cover the construction cost, the annual payment would be $22.9 million per year. The net cash flow is positive $14.9 million; therefore, the solar PV plant is feasible.

Over the 20-year planning horizon assuming 3% inflation for electricity prices and 2.5% inflation for O&M costs (Table 2), we predict it will continue to be profitable. Such conditions remain sensitive to electricity market prices, fuel prices, and inflation rate.

## Discussion

Compared to the NG power plant, the solar PV plant has lower operating cost and no fuel costs. The intermittency and variance of solar irradiance (Figure 2) may affect energy production and revenue. This uncertainty also applies to the wind farm. Unlike the wind and NG plants, the solar PV plant requires a significant amount of land area that may incur a large upfront cost for purchase or lease and may be accompanied with additional permitting obstacles and stakeholder opposition which could impact the project's feasibility.
