# L04: Wind Farm Feasibility Study

## Executive Summary

In this study, we evaluate the economic feasibility of a 50 MW wind farm over a 20-year outlook using dispatch logic based on hourly wind speed data and VT 2022 electricity prices.

Based on the interpolated GE 1.5 MW turbine power curve (Figure 1) and site wind data, the plant would require 65 GE 1.5 MW turbines to achieve a 50 MW annual average capacity, resulting in a total installed capacity of 97.5 MW. The calculated capacity factor is 0.51 with turbines producing power for 7863 hours per year.

In year 1, the plant would generate 6706 MW, producing $37.2 million in revenue from electricity sales. The plant would pay $4.1 million in O&M costs, with an annual capital payment of $AAA assuming a 20-year loan with 5% interest with principal $1.5 million/MW. The resulting year one profit is $11.8 million.

## Discussion

The modeled wind farm appears economically feasible under current price and cost assumptions. Compared to the previously modeled CHP NG power plant, the wind farm's economic performance is affected by the lack of fuel costs and large up-front capital investment. Policy incentives and production tax credits could improve profitability.
